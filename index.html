I Vision

Agents can plan an experiment. They still have nowhere to run it.

An AI agent can read the literature, form a hypothesis and write the protocol. Then the work stops, because the next step is physical. Someone has to pick up the tube, load the machine and watch what happens.

Robots have the opposite problem. The bodies are ready, but they learn by practice, and practice on a real bench is slow, costly and easy to break. A generic simulated room does not help: it is not your lab.

We build the missing place: your real space, rebuilt in simulation, accurate enough for an agent to work in it as if it were there.

II How it works

From photographs to a working environment, in three steps.

  1. i.

    Take pictures of the space

    It starts with a camera. The lab or the factory floor is photographed as it is: the benches, the instruments, and whatever was left on them.

  2. ii.

    We rebuild it in 3D, with physics

    The pictures become an accurate 3D environment in which every object is its own thing. It has weight and friction, and its lids and doors open on their hinges.

  3. iii.

    Agents command a robot inside it

    An agent sets up a task, tells a robot what to do, and reads what happened. It can try again as often as it needs to, because nothing here can break.

Not a generic simulated room. Yours, down to the bottle someone left on the bench.

III What it is for

One environment, two kinds of work.

Autonomous experiments

An agent plans an experiment, runs it with a robot, measures the result and decides what to try next. The loop closes without a person at the bench.

Training humanoids and robots

A robot practises the real task in the real layout, as many times as it takes, before it ever touches the actual equipment.
